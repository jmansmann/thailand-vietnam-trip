# Travel practicals

Research snapshot: 2026-10-20. Travel dates are Dec 27, 2026–Jan 9, 2027. Visa rules, entry forms, laws, event details, and embassy information can change; anything marked **verify** should be checked again before travel.

## Entry for U.S. passport holders

- **Thailand:** Current trip planning assumes visa-exempt tourist entry, but permitted stay length and eligibility are **verify** against Thai Immigration before booking onward travel. Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) is the official online arrival form; submit within the official permitted window (the project brief plans for within three days). Confirm any tourism fee by departure.
- **Vietnam:** Plan to obtain an **e-visa before travel**. Number of entries, validity, fee, processing time, required photo, passport validity, and named entry port are **verify** on the official government application portal; it was not retrievable in this research pass. Do not use a lookalike visa site or an agent without confirming that the application is filed with the Vietnamese immigration authority.
- **Passport:** Carry a passport with at least six months of validity beyond entry and spare blank pages unless current official rules confirm otherwise. Keep onward itinerary, hotel details, and approvals offline.
- **Singapore transit:** Confirm with the operating airline whether travelers remain airside and whether any transit / entry document is needed for terminal change, overnight stop, or baggage re-check. This trip does not include a planned Singapore stopover.
- **Official travel pages:** State Department country-information pages returned 403 during this research pass. Do not infer current visa terms from their failure; re-check the U.S. State Department and each destination's immigration authority before travel.

## Health and insurance

- Book a travel-clinic appointment at least a month before departure. Ask about routine vaccines, hepatitis A/B, typhoid, and whether Japanese encephalitis or rabies vaccination is appropriate for planned activities.
- **Mosquitoes:** Dengue risk is present in both countries year-round. Bring repellent, use it as directed, and cover up near dawn/dusk and around outdoor dining.
- **Water / food:** Use sealed bottled or treated water, be cautious with ice where hygiene is unclear, and wash hands before eating. Choose busy stalls with fresh turnover.
- **Animal contact:** Do not touch or feed street animals or monkeys; wash bites/scratches and seek medical care immediately. Prioritize travel insurance that covers medical treatment and evacuation.
- **Water activities:** Wear the provided life jacket, ask about sea conditions, and skip open-water swimming during unsafe weather. Avoid freshwater swimming if water conditions or sanitation are uncertain.
- Purchase travel medical insurance and evacuation cover; save the insurer's 24/7 number and policy offline.

## Money and connectivity

- **Currency:** Thai baht (THB) and Vietnamese đồng (VND). Exchange-rate placeholder for rough mental math only: **~฿35 / US$1** and **~₫25,000 / US$1**; use a current converter before withdrawal.
- Cards work at higher-end hotels/restaurants, but bring cash for markets, small vendors, tips, and small transport fares. Tell the bank about international use; check ATM fees and decline dynamic currency conversion.
- Tip for good service rather than assuming a fixed percentage: round up or leave a modest amount at cafés/restaurants, and tip private guides/drivers when service merits it. Bargain politely at markets; do not haggle over posted menu prices.
- eSIM is the lowest-friction option for five travelers; local SIMs at airports are an alternative. Confirm handset unlock, data coverage, and hotspot allowance.
- Install before departure: Grab, Bolt (Thailand), Google Maps offline areas, Google Translate offline Thai/Vietnamese, LINE, Zalo, Klook and a currency converter. Download hotel/booking confirmations and maps for offline use.
- **Power:** Both countries use 220V / 50Hz. Expect Type A/B/C/O sockets in Thailand and A/C/F in Vietnam. Bring a compact universal adapter; check each device's input voltage and hotel outlet type.

## Weather, packing, time zone

- **Phuket / Bangkok:** Hot and humid in daytime. Phuket is generally in its drier season; Bangkok is in the cooler/drier time of year, though both can have showers.
- **Hanoi / Lan Ha / Ninh Binh:** Winter can be cool, damp, and misty; pack a light insulated layer and rain shell. Kayaking and swimming can feel cold in January.
- **Carry-on + backpack:** breathable daytime clothes; light warm layer; rain shell; quick-dry shorts; modest temple clothes covering shoulders/knees; one NYE outfit; comfortable walking shoes; sandals; swimsuit; dry bag; sunscreen; insect repellent; personal medication; earplugs/sleep mask; spare glasses/contacts; charger, power bank, adapter, and passport/insurance photocopy.
- **Time:** Both countries are UTC+7 year-round, **12 hours ahead of New York City in winter**.

## Etiquette, laws, and safety

- Temples: cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes where requested, and do not point feet at Buddha images. Ask before photographing worshippers.
- In Thailand, avoid touching people's heads; keep feet off tables and never point feet at people or sacred objects. A **wai** is a respectful greeting, but a smile and slight nod are fine if unsure. Men commonly use **khrap** as a polite sentence ending.
- Never disrespect the Thai monarchy, including social-media posts or gestures involving currency or images of the king. Lèse-majesté is a serious criminal offence.
- Buddha images are sacred objects: do not climb on them, pose disrespectfully, or export religious artifacts without checking rules.
- **Drugs / cannabis:** Thai law and enforcement have been changing; do not buy, possess, or use cannabis or other drugs without checking current law. Never carry anything for another person.
- **Vapes:** E-cigarettes are prohibited or heavily restricted in Thailand and Vietnam; leave them at home. Verify current rules and penalties.
- **Alcohol:** Thai retail sale hours have changed through legal amendments and local restrictions. Do not plan around an old online timetable; confirm current hours and venue policy. Never drink and drive.
- Road safety: do not rent or drive a motorbike without a valid motorcycle licence, required IDP, helmet, and insurance. Prefer seat-belted cars and licensed drivers. Crossing Hanoi streets takes patience; walk predictably and avoid sudden stops.
- Nightlife: keep drinks in view, agree on a buddy/meeting point, use rideshare or a pre-booked driver, and carry only the cash/card needed.

## Scams to recognize

- **Thailand:** “Grand Palace is closed today” detours, gem/tuk-tuk commission trips, unmetered taxis, Phuket jet-ski damage claims, and pressure to leave a passport with a rental shop.
- **Vietnam:** fake e-visa portals, taxi meter manipulation, cyclo/shoe-shine/fruit-photo overcharging, and VND zero confusion. Agree on an unmetered fare in advance, check the app destination and vehicle plate, and count cash aloud.
- Keep passports secured except when an official check requires the original. Store a photocopy / phone scan separately.
